Ashok Gurung

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at Washington State University, where I work with Prof. Scott Beckman and Prof. Sinisa Mesarovic on multiscale and multiphysics modeling of functional ceramics. My current research focuses on developing scalable COMSOL-based simulation workflows for high-temperature sintering and joining of dissimilar materials under extreme conditions.

I earned my Ph.D. in Physics from the University of Connecticut, where I was advised by Prof. Serge Nakhmanson , and my M.S. in Physics from Wright State University. During my graduate research, I combined quantum-mechanical atomistic modeling with thermodynamics-based phase-field simulations to predict material properties and understand microstructure-driven performance.

More broadly, my research aims to connect atomistic, mesoscopic, and continuum-scale simulations to understand how coupled electrical, thermal, chemical, and mechanical phenomena govern microstructure evolution in diverse material systems, including solid-state batteries, functional ceramics, ferroelectrics, and relaxor dielectrics .

Code & Projects

Open-source tools and simulation results on GitHub.

Sintering-Multiphysics-Problem
COMSOL Multiphysics · Java API · SLURM/HPC · Python

Phase-field simulation of solid-state sintering coupled to vacancy diffusion, plus the tools to run it robustly on HPC clusters: checkpointed restart chains, license-aware batch launchers, and standard pore / neck / compaction metrics. Includes a four-grain simulation gallery showing neck growth and pore closure.

Electrostrictive_Mining
Python · Materials Project database

Code for mining the Materials Project database to screen candidate electrostrictive materials, used in the npj Computational Materials (2022) study above.
